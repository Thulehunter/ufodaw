How does UFODAW compare?

UFODAW is young, small and free. Other DAWs have had decades and whole companies behind them. Here is an honest look at where UFODAW holds its own, and where it does not (yet).

Where UFODAW shines

  • Free, for real. No trial, no account, no subscription, no licence key. Open source under the AGPLv3, so you can read, change and build it.
  • Light and easy to set up. A small installer (no administrator rights needed), and one .mdaw file that can carry all its audio to another disk or PC.
  • A lot built in for a 0.9. Loop-take comping, warping, sidechain, multiband compression, a LUFS meter, freeze, track groups, a clip launcher and plugin delay compensation up to two seconds.
  • Hard to break. A plugin that crashes is caught and switched off instead of taking your session with it. Autosave, crash recovery and a version history are on by default.
  • Predictable multi-core. Heavy projects mix on several cores, and the tests check the result is bit-identical to a single core.
  • Quick to drive. A command palette (Ctrl+K), fully rebindable shortcuts and a command-line render.

What is still missing

  • Windows only. No Linux build, and the macOS build is untested.
  • VST3 only. No VST2 and no CLAP, so older plugins will not load.
  • A small sound library. The built-in synth, drums and starter pack are decent, but nowhere near the instrument collections of FL Studio or Ableton Live Suite.
  • Young software. Version 0.9, one main developer, a small community, few tutorials, and some rough edges. Expect bugs.
  • No code signing yet, so Windows SmartScreen shows a warning on first run.
  • No scripting or extension API, no notation view, no video track, and no real-time collaboration.

Which one should you pick?

Choose UFODAW if…

You are on Windows, want a free and open DAW with no strings attached, work with VST3 plugins, and like a light program that stays out of your way. It suits beats, songwriting sketches, recording and mixing a small project, and anyone who wants to look under the hood or help shape it.

Choose REAPER if…

You want a very flexible, light and affordable DAW on any platform, with scripting and a huge community. You will bring your own instruments.

Choose FL Studio or Ableton Live if…

You want a large built-in sound library, polished instruments, a big pool of tutorials and the workflow that much of the industry uses. Live is the pick for clip-based, live performance.

Choose Cakewalk if…

You want a free, full-featured traditional DAW on Windows with notation and strong recording, and you do not mind that it is closed source.

Choose LMMS if…

You want a free, open-source, pattern-based beat maker that also runs on Linux and macOS, and you do not need to record audio.

Not sure?

Try it. UFODAW needs no account and costs nothing, so the only price is a few minutes. Keep your other DAW next to it and open the same plugins in both.

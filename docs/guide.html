The UFODAW guide

The everyday things are easy to find. This page explains the deeper features: what they do, how to use them, and the details that are easy to miss.

Getting started

Install

Run UFODAW-<version>-setup.exe (no administrator rights needed), or unzip the zip anywhere and start UFODAW.exe. Windows may show a SmartScreen message because the program is not code-signed yet: More info > Run anyway. The first start shows a short tour and writes the starter pack of sounds.

Set up your sound

Press Ctrl+K, type audio settings and press Enter. Pick your interface (the ASIO driver of your interface gives the lowest latency; Windows Audio works everywhere), the sample rate and the buffer size. A smaller buffer means less delay and more risk of crackles; 256 to 512 samples is a good start. The Recording latency tab places recordings exactly on the beat, and the CPU tab sets how many cores help with the mixing.

Your first minutes

  1. File > New from a template and choose Beat starter. Press Space to play and stop.
  2. Change something in the drum rack or the piano roll. Ctrl+Z undoes it; a template cannot be ruined, because it opens as a new project.
  3. Add a track with the + button on the left: an audio track (for recording and samples), a MIDI track with a synth or plugin, or a drum track.
  4. Record: arm an audio track, press R, play, press Space to stop. Turn on the count-in in the top bar if you like.
  5. Save with Ctrl+S; File > Export audio makes a WAV, FLAC or MP3 of the mix.

Lost? Ctrl+K is the command palette: type what you want to do (export, reverb, tempo) and it finds it. File > Quick tour shows the tour again.

Recording, takes and comping

Every audio track picks its own input (mono, or a stereo pair of your interface), so several armed tracks record at once, each from its own source. Mon lets you hear the input live through the track's effects, volume and pan. Use headphones or your interface's direct monitoring to avoid feedback and double latency.

Loop recording

  1. Set a loop region by right-dragging on the ruler, and turn on Loop.
  2. Press R. Every pass through the loop becomes a take; the newest one plays.
  3. Right-click the clip and choose Choose take to audition others.

Comping

Right-click a take and choose Comp takes. Every take gets its own row; swipe across a row to play that take at that spot. The takes are split at the swipe with short fades at the joins, and each swipe is a separate undo step. In the comp view each take is a block: drag an edge to trim it, Alt+drag the body to move it, right-click to duplicate or delete. Keep this take flattens the result.

Prefer the keyboard? Press S to cut all takes at the playhead, then pick a different take for each part.

Punch in/out

Turn on Punch (P). The region is the selected clip, or two bars at the playhead; change it with Shift+right-drag on the ruler. Press Record: playback starts a bar before the region, records inside it, and closes the recording itself at the punch-out. The rest of the take is untouched.

Count-in and click

Count-in gives 0–4 bars of clicks before recording starts. They follow the tempo and time signature at the playhead, play even when the metronome is off, and nothing is recorded during them. The Click button (M) toggles the metronome at any time.

Several inputs at once

Arm a keyboard track and an e-drum track with their red dots, set each track's MIDI input in its VST panel, and press Record: they record together, each from its own device and channel. This works alongside armed audio tracks too. Mod wheel, expression, pedals, pitch bend and aftertouch are recorded with the notes and edited in the lane under the piano roll.

Latency and audio setup

Sound needs time to travel to your headphones and back through the interface, so a recording arrives late. UFODAW places it on the timeline earlier by that round trip, so it lines up with what you played along to. This also applies to loop takes and punch recordings. Open the Audio icon for three tabs:

Automatic
Uses the input and output latency the driver reports.
Measured
Press Measure. UFODAW plays a short click out of the first two outputs and listens for it on the input you pick. Connect an output to that input with a cable (or point a microphone at the speakers), turn the monitors down, and measure. The result is remembered.
None
Switches compensation off.
Extra offset
Fine-tune in milliseconds (positive = earlier). Record a click or clap and check that it lands on the grid.

Drivers and ASIO

Out of the box you get Windows Audio (WASAPI), its Exclusive and Low Latency modes, and DirectSound. For ASIO drivers (PreSonus, Roland Rubix, AudioBox and others) a build has to include Steinberg's ASIO SDK, which cannot be shipped with the sources. If you build UFODAW yourself:

  1. Download the SDK from Steinberg and accept their licence.
  2. Unzip it into third_party\ so that third_party\asiosdk_<version>\common\iasiodrv.h exists.
  3. Run CMake again. It prints ASIO support: ON and ASIO shows up as a driver type.

Windows lets a program use one ASIO driver at a time. To use two interfaces together, choose Windows Audio (input and output can be different devices) or an aggregate driver from the interface maker.

Automation

Press A on a track and pick what to automate: volume, pan, any built-in effect parameter, the synth or sampler, or any parameter of a hosted VST3 plugin. Click in the lane to add breakpoints, drag to move, right-click to remove. Pan envelopes honour Pan snap 5%.

Recording rides

  1. Pick an envelope, then Record rides and a mode: Touch, Latch or Write.
  2. Press play and move the fader or knob.
  3. Every pass is kept as a take and comped into the envelope with a short crossfade.

In the lane, Shift+drag selects a time range, and right-click offers Play here (per take), Delete take and Fade between takes. Comp view shows the result on top and every take in its own row; swipe across a row and the result updates at once.

The sampler and slicing

Choose + > Sampler for a MIDI track that plays one sample. Load a file (or drop one on the track), drag the yellow start and end markers, and pick a mode:

Classic
Pitched by the key relative to the root note, with loop, ADSR and a low-pass filter. Detect pitch sets the root note and tuning from the sample itself.
One-shot
Plays out completely, whatever the key does.
Slice
The region is cut into equal slices, one key each.

Slice by hits

In Slice mode, Slice by hits cuts the region at its transients (kicks, snares, hats) instead of in equal parts. The slider next to it sets the sensitivity. To MIDI places the slices on the track as a pattern, one note per slice from the slice key upward, in the original timing, at the playhead. That is the quickest way to rearrange a drum loop.

Tempo tools

Tempo… tells you how many beats the marked part lasts and what tempo that makes. Detect the tempo finds it from the audio. Then either set the project tempo to it, or stretch the sample (pitch kept) to the project's tempo.

Right-click any audio clip and choose Sample this clip to turn it into a sampler track with one note. The Stretch slider (0.25x–4x) changes length without changing pitch, and works on drum pads too.

Warping and stretching audio

Right-click an audio clip and choose Warp…. The clip is drawn over the project's beat grid.

  1. Find hits places a marker on every transient.
  2. Match tempo brings the clip to the project tempo.
  3. Snap to grid moves every marker to the nearest sixteenth.
  4. Drag markers to fine-tune: double-click adds one, right-click removes one.
  5. Apply stretches the audio between markers with pitch kept, in the background. It is saved with the project and can be undone.

Simple stretch and pitch

Gain, stretch and pitch… sets clip gain (with Normalize), length as a percentage (200% = twice as long, or Fit to project tempo from the clip's own BPM), and pitch in semitones. It is rendered offline with Rubber Band from the original, so Reset always restores the untouched audio, and results are cached on disk. Edit volume curve draws a multiplier over the clip; splitting the clip splits the curve too.

Drum rack and sub-tracks

The Drum Rack has 16 pads, each a small sampler with start/end, pitch, envelope, filter, reverse, level, pan, mute, solo and a choke group (closed and open hi-hat). With the pad window focused, Z X C V / A S D F / Q W E R / 1 2 3 4 play the pads. A step sequencer plays all 16.

Velocity layers and round-robin

A pad can hold up to three more samples, each with a velocity range. Samples with overlapping ranges take turns, so a repeated snare never sounds identical. Ranges side by side make a soft/hard pair.

Sub-tracks for drum plugins

Load a drum plugin (EZdrummer, Superior Drummer, Addictive Drums, BFD…) and its drum sub-tracks appear by themselves: one lane per pitch (kick, snare, hats) under the track. You can also create them from the notes the track uses, from a General MIDI preset, or by hand. Each sub-track has its own mute, solo, name, pitch range, a volume fader (0–1.5, scaling the velocity of that pitch) and a small meter showing how hard it is hit. Click an empty spot in a lane to add a note and a note to remove it. Everything is saved with the project and undoable.

Stretchable patterns

Drag the right edge of a MIDI or drum clip: longer repeats the pattern, shorter cuts it. Hold Shift to stretch the pattern itself (half as long = twice as fast), and Alt to switch snapping off.

MIDI routing and sequencer plugins

A sequencer plugin such as Stochas makes notes but no sound. UFODAW lets it play the synth of one or more other tracks.

  1. Add two MIDI tracks: one for the sequencer, one for the synth.
  2. On the synth track, open VST and pick your synthesizer.
  3. On the sequencer track, open VST, set Use as to Sequencer, and pick the plugin.
  4. Tick the track(s) that should play its notes. Each ticked track has a channel setting: All channels passes notes unchanged; Channel N sends only that channel, delivered as channel 1.
  5. Press play. The plugin receives tempo, bar position and play state from the DAW. The sequencer track itself is silent.

Because each channel can go to a different synth, one multi-track sequencer can drive a whole rack of instruments.

An arpeggiator or chord tool that should change the notes of one track belongs under MIDI effects in that track's VST panel instead.

Quantize, swing, humanize

Press Q on a MIDI clip: grid from 1/4 to 1/32 and triplets, strength, swing (100% = triplet feel), random timing (ms) and velocity variation, optionally note lengths. It works on drum clips too.

Hardware synths and MIDI clock

Playing an external synth

  1. Connect the interface's (or a USB) MIDI OUT to the synth's MIDI IN, and set the synth to the channel you send on.
  2. In a MIDI or sequencer track's VST panel, tick the MIDI output under External MIDI instruments (hardware).
  3. From channel chooses which sequencer channel goes there; Send as channel is what the hardware receives.
  4. Press play. Notes leave shifted by the output latency so the synth stays in time, and all notes off is sent when you stop.

A track that sends to hardware makes no sound itself and gives no stem. To hear the synth in the DAW, connect its audio to an interface input and record it on an audio track. A sequencer can drive internal synths and several hardware synths at once.

MIDI clock

Under Audio… > MIDI clock, tick the outputs of drum machines or sequencers that should follow UFODAW. They get 24 ticks per quarter note following the tempo map, Start (or Continue with the song position), a new position when you jump, and Stop. Clock shift nudges the clock a few milliseconds to line hardware up with the audio. Exports never play hardware or send clock.

MIDI learn, scales and tap tempo

MIDI learn

  1. Open File > MIDI controllers (MIDI learn), or find it in the command palette.
  2. Add a mapping and choose a track and what to control: volume, pan, mute, solo, or any effect, synth, sampler or plugin parameter that can be automated.
  3. Press Learn and move a knob or fader on your controller.

Mappings are saved with the project.

Key and chords in the piano roll

Left of the keys, pick a root note and a scale (major, minor, the modes, pentatonics, blues and more). The rows of the scale stay light and the others are dimmed; the root is tinted and the scale's keys get a dot. The setting is stored with the track.

Snap to scale
New and moved notes jump to the nearest note of the scale.
Fit notes
Moves the notes of the clip that are outside the scale.
Triads / 7th chords on click
Adds a whole chord with one click. The chord is built from the scale, so each degree gets the right kind of chord.

Tap tempo

Press the Tap button next to the tempo, or the key T, in time with the music. The project tempo follows your taps.

Buses, sends and sidechain

Buses and sends

+ > Bus creates a group or effects-return channel. Put a reverb or delay on it, then use the Out button on any track: play the track into the bus (a drum bus, say) and/or send part of it with a level per bus, post-fader or Pre. Muting a track mutes its sends; soloing a track keeps its bus audible.

Sidechain compression

In a track's FX panel, the compressor has a Listens to box. Pick another track (the kick) and this track ducks whenever that one is loud, so a bass or pad pumps with the kick. Picking a track turns the compressor on with fitting settings. The track being listened to can be muted and still does its job, and both stems and the mix contain the ducking.

Plugin delay compensation

Tracks are delayed to line up with plugins that report latency (up to two seconds), so a lookahead limiter on one track does not smear it against the others. If a plugin reports a different latency while playing, the compensation crossfades over about 10 ms, so there is no gap or click.

Mono check

The Mono button on any track, bus or the master sums left and right. Use it to check a source or the whole mix in mono, or to centre a stereo recording.

Plugins and CPU

VST3 instruments and effects open in their own windows and are saved with the project. A track name turns yellow at 25% and orange at 50% of the audio time, so you can see which plugin is heavy; hover the header for the exact figure. The status line shows the device load.

The effects chain

Open a track's FX panel. Along the top is the effects chain, in the order the sound goes through it: gate, EQ, compressor, de-esser, saturation, multiband compressor, chorus/flanger, delay, reverb and limiter. The light on a tile switches that effect on or off; click the tile to see its controls below. Every effect has presets, and every parameter can be automated and MIDI-learned.

Six-band parametric EQ

A 24 dB/oct high-pass, low shelf, two peaks (with Q), high shelf and a 24 dB/oct low-pass, drawn as one frequency response curve with a node per band. Drag a node sideways for the frequency and up or down for the gain, scroll over a peak to change its Q, and double-click to reset a band. The selected band also has sliders next to the curve, and a live spectrum of the sound is drawn behind it while the panel is open.

Compressor

Soft knee
A gentle transition into compression instead of an abrupt one.
Peak / RMS
Choose how the level is detected: fast peaks, or the average loudness.
Sidechain filter
A high-pass on the detector, so low bass does not trigger the compression.
Dry/wet mix
Blend the compressed and original signal for parallel compression.
Auto makeup
Compensates the level lost by compressing.
Presets
Vocal, drums, bass, bus glue, parallel, limiter and ducking.

A live transfer curve and a gain-reduction meter show what the compressor is doing. The Listens to box still gives you sidechain ducking from another track.

Delay

Separate left and right times, a low and high cut inside the feedback so repeats get darker or thinner, ping-pong, tape-style wobble, and tempo sync with note lengths.

Reverb

An 8-line feedback delay network with decay time, size, pre-delay, damping, low cut, width and motion. Presets cover room, plate, hall and cathedral. Projects from older versions load as before.

Gate

Closes when the sound stays under the threshold, with hysteresis (it closes a few dB lower than it opens, so it does not chatter), attack, hold and release, and a range for how far it turns the sound down. The detector can be filtered with a low and a high cut, so bleed from other instruments does not open it. A meter shows how closed it is.

De-esser

Listens to a band around the sibilance frequency (with an adjustable width) and lowers only that band when it is loud, or the whole sound in Whole sound mode. Listen plays the band the detector hears, so you can sweep the frequency until the harsh sounds are loudest.

Multiband compressor

Three bands with Linkwitz-Riley splits that add up flat again. Every band has its own threshold and gain; ratio, attack and release are shared. A meter per band shows what each is doing.

Saturation

Five characters (tube, tape, hard clip, wave fold and diode), drive, bias (adds even harmonics), a tone tilt and a dry/wet mix. 4x oversampling runs the clipping at four times the sample rate, which removes most of the aliasing; the sound is delayed 12 samples and the track is compensated.

Chorus and flanger

One to four modulated voices per side, width, a tone filter and feedback. A delay of 10-25 ms with a mild depth is a chorus; 1-5 ms with feedback is a flanger; mix all the way up with one voice is a vibrato.

Limiter

A lookahead limiter: the gain comes down before a peak arrives, so nothing is clipped and nothing pumps. The track is delayed by the lookahead and the program compensates for that. True peak also catches the peaks between the samples.

Freeze, groups and plugin safety

Freeze

Right-click a track > Freeze bounces the track (instrument, effects, plugins, volume and pan) to audio on a new track right after it and switches the original off completely, so its plugins no longer use the CPU. A frozen track has a yellow frame. Unfreeze removes the bounce and plays the track itself again. Freeze is one undo step.

Track groups (folders)

Right-click a track > Group (folder) to start a new group with it or move it into an existing one. The group gets a header row with a name, an arrow that collapses its tracks, and mute and solo for all of them together. Double-click the header to rename it; right-click it to ungroup. Groups are saved with the project.

When a plugin misbehaves

  • Scanning runs in a helper process (UFODAW starts itself as the helper), so a plugin that crashes while it is examined no longer takes the program down.
  • Loading: a plugin that crashed the program while it was loaded is skipped the next time, so the project opens without it. Open the Plugins window to allow it again.
  • Playing: a plugin that crashes while processing sound is caught and switched off. An effect lets the sound through, an instrument goes silent, and a message names the plugin. Save and reopen the project to load it again.

Clip launcher

The clip launcher is for playing and trying ideas live instead of laying them out on the timeline. Open it from File > Clip launcher or the command palette (Ctrl+K). The Launcher jam template (File > New from a template) is a project with four rows of drums, bass, chords and a lead, ready to play.

The grid

Every track is a column and every idea is a row (a "scene"). Click a cell to start its clip; click a row number to start the whole row. A clip repeats until you start another cell of the same track or press the small stop square under the track name. Stop all ends everything. The transport starts by itself, and stops ending the clips.

Staying in time

A start or stop waits for the next bar by default, so clips always fall on the beat. The box at the top changes that to the next beat or to at once. While a start is waiting, the cell blinks; a playing cell shows its position in the loop.

Filling cells

Right-click a cell: Load an audio file (audio tracks), New 4-bar pattern (MIDI tracks), or Use the clip that is selected in the arrangement (with or without taking it off the arrangement). To edit a clip, right-click it and choose Move to the arrangement: it lands at the playhead and the cell is emptied; put it back the same way. + Row adds rows. The cells are saved in the project.

Clips in the grid are not on the timeline: they play on their own while the transport runs, next to what is on the timeline. The loop region of the arrangement does not repeat them.

Starter pack, templates and the sample browser

The starter pack

On first start the program writes its own sounds to %APPDATA%\UFODAW\StarterPack: about 45 drum one-shots (kicks, snares, claps, hats, toms, cymbals, percussion), bass and synth sounds, effects (risers, an impact, vinyl crackle) and four drum loops with their tempo in the name. They are synthesised by the program, so you can use them in any music without worrying about licences.

Drum kits and synth sounds

The drum rack has a box at the top that fills all 16 pads from a kit: Punch, 808, Lo-fi, House and Tight. Undo brings your own samples back. In the piano roll of a synth track, the Sounds box sets the wave, the envelope and the filter: pluck, warm pad, strings, brass, lead, keys, bell, sub bass and fat bass.

New from a template

File > New from a template opens a finished starting point as a new, unsaved project, so the template itself is never overwritten: Launcher jam (for the clip launcher), Beat starter, Lo-fi hip hop, House 124, Trap 140, Song sketch (named sections and empty tracks for guitar and vocals) and Vocal recording (a vocal track with a ready effects chain and a reverb bus, and the click on).

The sample browser

Open it from the File menu or the command palette (Ctrl+K). It lists the starter pack by category and any folders you add. Click a sound to hear it (through the master output, apart from your project); double-click, or press Add to project, to put it on a new audio track; or drag it onto a track, a drum pad or the sampler. The search box looks in all folders.

Updates

File > Check for updates asks GitHub for the latest release. Only that one small request is made; nothing about you or your projects is sent. When a newer version exists, an orange Update available button appears in the top bar with what is new; Install now updates the program for you (see Updates & problem reports). The program also checks once a day at start-up; you can switch that off in the same menu.

Metering and reference tracks

The Meter button on the master strip shows loudness in LUFS (momentary, short-term, integrated, measured as streaming services do: ITU-R BS.1770 / EBU R128), the highest sample, a 20 Hz–20 kHz spectrum and a target to aim for: −14 LUFS for Spotify and YouTube, −16 for Apple Music, −23 for broadcast.

A/B against a reference

  1. In the master meter, press Load reference… and choose a finished song.
  2. Start here lines it up at the playhead.
  3. Match loudness sets it to the loudness of your mix so louder does not sound better.
  4. Press B to switch between your mix and the reference while playing, with a short crossfade.

The reference is saved with the project and is never part of an export.

Clipping lamps

A red CLIP lamp lights in the top bar, on every meter and as a red frame on the strip when a track output, the master (over 0 dB before the limiter) or an armed input reaches full scale. They stay lit until clicked. A recording that clips cannot be repaired, so lower the gain on the interface.

Tempo map and sections

Tempo (next to BPM) opens a list of sections, each starting at a bar with its own BPM and time signature (3/4, 6/8, 7/8…). The ruler, grid, snapping, metronome and the tempo given to plugins all follow it. Changing tempo or meter keeps MIDI, the loop and the punch region in place in the music (same bar, same beat), while audio clips stay where they are in time.

Arrangement sections

The Sections button names parts of the song (verse, chorus…) as coloured bands on the ruler. Go moves the playhead, Loop loops the part, Copy duplicates it with everything inside it and moves later material along, and < / > swap neighbours. Clip and Paste section move a whole section between projects; tracks that do not exist yet (by name) are created.

Exporting and stems

Export audio and Export stems offer WAV (16 / 24 / 32-bit float), FLAC, AIFF, MP3 (128–320 kbps) and Ogg Vorbis. Rendering happens in the background with a Cancel button, and your last format is remembered.

Export stems writes one file per track into a folder you pick, named 01 - Bass loop.wav, 02 - Synth 1.wav and so on, in track order.

  • Each stem is the track as you hear it: after effects and plugins, with volume, pan and automation.
  • Stems are dry: they ignore buses and sends, so a shared reverb is not included. Use Export audio for the full mix.
  • Muted, empty and sequencer tracks are skipped, and the summary tells you which.
  • 32-bit float cannot clip, even when a stem goes above 0 dBFS.
  • By default the stems add up exactly to the mix. Optionally each stem can get the master volume and soft limiter.
  • Every stem is as long as the whole project plus reverb and delay tails, so they line up in any other DAW.

Projects, tabs and safety

  • Project check. When a project opens, one dialog lists what did not come through: missing audio files, plugins that could not load, or a project saved by a newer UFODAW. Find the files... searches a folder (and its subfolders) for the missing files by name and opens the project again with them in place; save to keep the new places.
  • Project tabs. Open several projects at once (Ctrl+N, Ctrl+W, Ctrl+Tab). All tabs share one audio device; only the visible tab is connected to it and to MIDI, so switching is instant. Unsaved tabs have a yellow dot.
  • Copy between projects. The clipboard is shared by all tabs.
  • Autosave. Every two minutes, when something changed, a recovery file is written. After a crash, the next start offers to restore it.
  • Backups and version history. Every save leaves a time-stamped copy (the newest twenty are kept), plus one every ten minutes of unsaved work. File > Version history… opens one in a new tab or goes back to it, keeping the current state as a copy first.
  • Save As. Save a new version (jam, jam v2, jam v3…) or Save with all audio in one folder, which copies every audio file into <folder>Audio so the project can move to another disk or PC.
  • Track templates. Right-click a track header > Save as a template… keeps its settings, effects, plugins with state and clips. + > From a template adds it to any project.
  • STOP ALL (Ctrl+Shift+Space) stops playback and recording and silences every sound at once, plugins and MIDI hardware included.

Under the hood, the audio thread never blocks: the interface edits the project under a lock that the audio callback only tries to take. Undo uses cheap snapshots of the whole project, where sample data is shared rather than copied.

Updates & problem reports

Updating

When a newer version exists, an orange Update available button appears in the top bar (the program asks GitHub once a day at start-up; File > Check for updates asks right now, and you can switch the daily check off in the same menu). The button shows what is new. Install now downloads the installer, checks it against the checksum GitHub lists for it, closes UFODAW, updates and starts it again. Unsaved changes are kept in the autosave and offered when the program starts. If anything does not match, nothing is installed and you are pointed to the download page. Download page opens the releases page instead.

Reporting a problem

File > Report a problem asks what went wrong and then shows the complete report. It holds the UFODAW version, the Windows version, the processor and memory, the time, and what you typed. Nothing is sent by the program: Report on GitHub opens a prefilled issue page that you read and submit yourself, and Copy report puts the text on the clipboard (for an e-mail or a chat). A copy is also kept in %APPDATA%\UFODAW\reports.

After a crash

If the program is closed by a crash, the next start says so and shows the same kind of report, with the file the crash happened in (your user folder name is replaced by %USERPROFILE%). If that file belongs to a plugin, the plugin is blocked so the project can open again without it; open Plugins to allow it again. Your project is offered back from the autosave. No project data, audio or file names of your projects are ever in a report.

Troubleshooting

No sound

Open Audio settings and press Test. Check that the right output is selected and that no other program has the interface in exclusive mode. Check that the master and the track are not muted and that STOP ALL is not lit.

Crackles or dropouts

Raise the buffer size in Audio settings. Look at the CPU figure in the status line and at the track names (yellow at 25% and orange at 50% of the audio time): freeze heavy tracks, or choose more helpers in the CPU tab. Close other programs that use the interface.

Recordings are late

Use the Recording latency tab: measure the round trip (it plays a click and listens for it on the input, so connect an output to an input with a cable first) or add an offset by hand.

A plugin is missing or blocked

Open Plugins and scan again (scanning runs in a helper process, so a bad plugin cannot take the program down). Blocked plugins are allowed again when you open that window. Only VST3 plugins are supported.

The program closed unexpectedly

Start it again and accept the restore prompt. Send the report that is offered; it tells which file crashed.

Windows shows a SmartScreen warning

The program is not code-signed yet. Choose More info > Run anyway.

Where are my files?

Settings, autosaves, the starter pack and reports are in %APPDATA%\UFODAW. Your projects are where you saved them; File > Save with all audio in one folder makes a project that can be moved to another computer.

Known limitations

  • Delay compensation covers audio plugins (instrument and effect inserts) up to 2 s. When a plugin reports another latency while playing, the compensation crossfades over about 10 ms.
  • What you do inside a plugin's own window is one undo step, taken when you open the window. Plugins that keep state outside what they save (a sample loaded by hand, say) may not go back completely.
  • Effect and plugin parameter automation is applied every 64 samples (volume and pan are ramped); synth parameters once per audio block. While a parameter is automated, the envelope wins during playback.
  • The crash protection covers a plugin while it processes sound and in its own window: its windows close, it is blocked and named, and the program goes on. If the program is closed anyway, that plugin is blocked at the next start and the autosave offers to restore your work. Heavy projects are mixed on several CPU cores (Audio settings, CPU tab).
  • VST3 on Windows. VST2 cannot be hosted (Steinberg no longer licenses it) and CLAP is not supported by the hosting code UFODAW is built on. A macOS build can host Audio Units, but it is untested.
  • Windows only so far.
